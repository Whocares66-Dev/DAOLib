# Examples

Small plugins, one per part of DAOLib, written to be read and copied from. Each is also a check of its part in the game: `tools\deploy.ps1 -Examples <part>` puts it in its own folder in `bin_ship\daolib\plugins\` with the rest (`-Examples *` for all of them), and each logs what it does to `example_<part>.log` in the daolib log folder (`Documents\BioWare\Dragon Age\daolib\`). They go in only when named, since each changes the game in its own way: `camera/` moves the camera, and would fight another plugin that does. A deploy that does not name one takes it out.

They build with the library when DAOLib is the top-level project (`DAO_BUILD_EXAMPLES`), and each is one `add_daolib_plugin` line in `examples/CMakeLists.txt`, as a plugin in its own repository would be (`dev/PLUGINS.md`).

To see one work: deploy it, play, and read its log. For `saves/`, save twice, load the first save, and save again: the log counts 1, 2, then back to 1 on the load and 2 on the next save. A new game logs that it counts from nothing. For `speed/`, start a fight: everything but the menus should move twice as fast until it ends. For `clock/`, get the controlled creature poisoned or burning in a fight: its ticks come half as often as anyone else's, and the log's time left on its effects runs down at half the rate; when the fight ends the log says how many timers were given back. For `commands/`, give the controlled creature orders and fight: each start and end is logged, failures for anyone. For `cooldown/`, use an ability from the quickbar: the log has the cooldown the game gave it, and the slot's sweep should then stand two thirds full and barely move. For `preview/`, move the cursor over the ground in explore or combat mode: a ring follows it, the log saying in reach (within 10 m of the controlled creature) or too far. For `dialogue/`, talk to someone with choices: the log has each line, the replies in their order, and those not offered. For `world/`, walk about and point at creatures, doors and chests: the log says whether the controlled creature can see each, the walk the pathfinder finds to it and the room it is in, and every two seconds what lies beneath the creature; every ten seconds it logs the walks to the six creatures nearest, so the log has them with no one at the mouse. For `damage/`, fight: its log gives each party member's hit and the half taken. For `messages/`, press an ability that needs a target on the quickbar, then Escape, then press it again and click an enemy: the log has the ability, the cancel and the click; drag an ability onto a quickbar slot, open the skills screen, and take a talent on the level-up screen: the slot, the creature, the ability and the screen's mode are in their lines. Select party members by F1 to F4, with Shift, and by clicking; open the party picker and a follower's tactics screen, change a preset and the behaviour: each is a line with its fields. Equip and unequip, give a follower a gift, craft, take from a chest, buy and sell at a store: the item, the creature and the numbers are in each line. Choose a reply, answer a script's popup, and open the journal from a quest's alert: the reply, the popup, its button and owner, and the quest are in theirs. For `levelup/`, level a party member up and close a screen: the log has its points and each page's learnable abilities, the same as the level-up screen shows. For `worldmap/`, leave an area by its exit to the world map: the log lists the map's pins as it shows them, the party's own marked, and the refusal of each pin not active. For `loot/`, open a chest or a body: the panel flashes and closes, its items in the backpack, and the log names the container. For `moments/`, load a save and fight: the load's end comes once with the area's tag, each death with its killer about two seconds before the scripts' death event, and opening and closing the inventory gives input back. For `party/`, switch who you control by the party bar and by key, equip and unequip in the inventory, and equip a two-handed weapon over a shield: each change is a line, the shield's coming off before the weapon's going on. For `camera/`, select another party member, close in and in the tactical view: the camera glides over, and close in comes round behind them. Bind its wide, narrow and free view keys in the controls screen: the views widen and narrow, and the free view takes the camera where it is and turns it slowly until pressed again. For `atmosphere/`, stand outdoors: over a minute the sky darkens to a purple night and the ground goes moonlit blue, the party's shadows fading, and over the next it all comes back, once; the log has the share of night every ten seconds and says when the area has its own look again. For `patch/`, walk through the Denerim market: no stutter as the crowd moves, and distant people keep their full detail; open the inventory: the character is sharp, with smooth edges. For `lights/`, stand outdoors in the day: shade under eaves and on walls away from the sun goes from near black to a dim blue, the figures unchanged; the log counts level pieces and moving things every ten seconds. By night in Redcliffe's village the fires' glow on the walls about them is fainter and the figures darker than vanilla's, the portraits as bright as ever. Walk a figure under a cliff or into a house's shadow by day: it stays lit by the sun, and the log counts every ten seconds the shares of the sun set and how many vanilla had in the shade. For `emitters/`, stand outdoors by night in Redcliffe's village: mist and smoke fainter, the fires as bright as ever; the log names each emitter, its model, whether the model has a light and where it is, and every ten seconds the area's running effects, each with its row, model, owner and place. For `textures/`, load a level and leave it: the log names its lightmaps with their sizes as they are made, then freed, and made less freed is always the count alive.

Writing one is also a review of the API it uses. Where an example is awkward to write or has a trap in it, the library is changed rather than the example worked around: `saves/` is why `dao/Saves.h` has `OnGameLoaded` (which also tells a plugin of a new game, so no state leaks from one playthrough into the next) and `Read`/`Write` (so no plugin writes file boilerplate or leaves half a file in a save); `camera/` is why `dao/Camera.h` has `Current()` (one call for the camera in the modes it moves in) and `OnTargetChanged` (selection as an event rather than a target polled every frame); `damage/` is why `CommandCall` has `SetInt`, `SetFloat` and `SetObject` (changing an argument before the command runs, which only an answer could be before); `speed/` is why `CClientExoApp::Mode` gives nothing, rather than a crash, before the game's modes exist, and `CurrentMode()` asks from anywhere; `world/` is why `CClientExoApp::CurrentExplore()` exists (explore mode in one call, rather than three null checks), and why `LineOfSight` answers a `Sight` that converts to bool and says where the view was cut, rather than an out parameter. `atmosphere/` is why the library reads a sky at all (the script commands only set one, a number at a time), why the sunlight has an `AreaOwnSunlight` and a `ResetSunlight` though the engine keeps no copy of it (the library keeps what its first write went over), why `dao/Framebuffer.h` reads an effect's state (the example's first version, the harness of DAOLib #62, ended a grade by turning the effect off, which left every area a third darker than a load does, since the game has it on with values no command gives), and why `dao::script::Table()`, the command table, became `CommandTable()`: beside it the name `dao::script::Table`, the data tables' enum, could not be said.

`src/plugins/example/` is older: one plugin that checks many parts of the library at once in play. What it shows moves here, one part at a time.
