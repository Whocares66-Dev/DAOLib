# What to reverse engineer next

Dragon Age's gameplay rules are mostly scripts and data. An attack's result is worked out in script (`rules_core.nss` handles `EVENT_TYPE_COMMAND_PENDING` through `Combat_HandleCommandAttack`, and `combat_h.nss` and `combat_damage_h.nss` hold the hit and damage formulas), and costs, ranges and damage values are rows of the data tables. Both are open to modders already: the toolset compiles scripts, and the override folder takes scripts and tables. Rebalancing and new formulas are done there, and DAOLib need not reach for them.

DAOLib earns its place where those cannot reach:

- **Engine state scripts cannot read or write.** No script command gives an effect's duration, for one.
- **Engine behaviour scripts cannot change.** The camera, input, time, the UI, movement and the command queue are examples.
- **Where script overrides do not compose.** A mod that changes `combat_h.nss` must recompile the 28 scripts that include it and ship them all, and two such mods cannot both be installed. A native plugin can change what those scripts do at the boundary instead (their script commands, their events), and several plugins can do it at once.

Each item says why it needs native work, what is known, and when it is done. Most valuable first. A finished item moves to `dev/LIBRARY.md`'s table, and gets an example in `examples/` when it is something a plugin would use (`dev/PLUGINS.md` says how). Refreshed 2026-10-04: much below was read on 2026-09-30 and has been built and played since, and the turn-based mod's needs of that week (attack reach, the melee ring, script timers, held time, the camera's distance) were met as they came (DAOLib #14 to #19).

## 1. The engine's messages, typed

- **Why native:** every message class a plugin can read is a hook point (`dao/Messages.h`). 11 of the 296 have fields in C++.
- **Leads:** `dev/messages.csv`, with each class's size from its clone (`tools/messages.py`); each handler's reads.
- **Abilities, typed (2026-10-24, DAOLib #253):** the targeting messages (OnTargetUnderCursor, OnSelectedTarget, OnPlayer, CancelAbilityTargeting, the pad's two), SetQuickslot, Show and HideAbilityHeader, CharGenAssignAbility, FindAbilityChains and CharGenSelectSpecialization, each logged by `examples/messages` in play but the last, which nothing sends; PerformAbility's third word read again as the ability's item. RequestTarget and IsValidTarget have no handler and no sender. Next: the party, inventory and dialogue.
- **Done when:** the messages for abilities, the party, inventory and dialogue are typed, and each field has been seen in play.
- **Asked for (2026-10-04, by the Eluvian server):** who has control and when it changes, and what goes on or comes off whom. Both are met by events at the engine's own functions rather than by typed messages, since a message catches only the player's own clicks (DAOLib #22: `dao::party::OnControlChanged`, `dao::equipment::OnEquip` and `OnUnequip`), seen in play 2026-10-04 and wired into the Eluvian server's bus. Nothing else asked for; the rest waits for a plugin that needs it.

## 2. World queries

- **Why native:** line of sight, path length and what lies between two points are engine questions. Scripts have a little of this, but not the pathfinder's path or a raycast.
- **Leads:** `World::Raycast` (`0x006497A0`, arguments read, `dao/Addresses.h`); the pick's hits (`dao/GameModes.h`, `PickHit`); explore mode's walkable test (`0x0070C840`) and the area's check (`0x0073DE70`).
- **Done when:** `dao::world::Raycast(from, to)` and a path length agree with what the game does in play.
- **Since (2026-10-01 to 10-04):** `FindPath` and `Pathfind` used in play by the turn-based mod and the Eluvian server; line of sight seen; a door's stamp and the stuck timer read (`dao/Addresses.h`). Left: naming the rest of the groups mask.
- **Done in code, not yet seen in play (2026-09-30):** `dao/World.h`: `Raycast` (the engine's `World::Raycast`, every shape along a ray), `FirstHit`, `LineOfSight` (the area's own test, `0x0067C3F0`, which the scripts' `CheckLineOfSight*` commands use, so doors block and chests do not), `CurrentArea`; `CCObject::Position`, `AreaId`, `Area`; `CClientExoApp::GetModule`, `CCModule::CurrentArea`. The areas' physics is PhysX; a query's mask is PhysX's groups mask, and which bit is which kind of object is read only in part (`dao::world::groups`). `examples/world` logs what it finds. Left: seeing it in play, naming the rest of the groups from its log, and a path length.
- **The pathfinder, read so far (2026-09-30):** the engine's `Pathfinding` namespace has a search environment per level of a hierarchy (`MapSearchEnvironment`, `MapAbstractionSearchEnvironment`, `MapSuperAbstractionSearchEnvironment`, vtables at `0x00B592E0`, `0x00B592F4`, `0x00B592CC`), so an A* over abstractions of the walkmesh. A search is a request advanced a step at a time (`0x00A4E1B0`: its state at the request's start, an iteration count at `+0x24`, the waypoints gathered through `0x00A48380`), not one call that returns a path. `0x00A46760` asks whether a straight line between two points is walkable (`0x00A4BB80`), and `0x00A62C90` uses it to cut corners from a creature's path (its waypoints, 12 bytes each, a vector at the path object's `+0x74`). The move-to-object command's handler is `0x007AA560`, reached through a table of handlers by command type; it picks the destination (`0x007A6CA0`) and the creature's movement asks for the path. Next: find where a request is made and stepped to its end, to give `dao::world::Path(from, to)` either by running the steps to completion on the game's thread or by reading the path a creature is given. The grid's tests, a blocked start or goal and spawning are in `dev/PATHFINDING.md` (2026-10-18).
- **Seen in play, 2026-09-30 (the second play test):** `CurrentPath` read a walked path right (5 waypoints, 10.33 m, what was left falling as the creature walked). A `FindPath` that called `RequestPath` until it answered was taken out: `RequestPath` takes new ends only when no search is in progress (`0x0073D6E0`), so its answers came a call late, a search it left unfinished held one answer through a whole fight, and the search it left stood as the creature's path, after which the creature would not walk a click. A path on demand needs a search object of the plugin's own (the `Pathfinding` environments over the area's mesh) or the creature's search saved and put back.
- **A search of the library's own (2026-09-30), not yet seen in play:** the movement controller is a plain record (the pathfinding world, the mover's parameters, its path, the two ends), and the script command `IsLocationSafe` makes one on its stack, so `dao::world::FindPath(creature, to, toward)` makes its own with the creature's size and follows `RequestPath`'s order: the destination made a free place, the ends set, the mover's own obstacle lifted, a straight walk tried, then the search in slices until it answers. The creature's own search is not touched. `examples/world` logs a walk to whatever is under the cursor. Seen in play 2026-10-01, after a second version that moves the search's walker along and counts a path only if it arrives (`dao/World.h`). The search's answers as read and seen: 4 a coarse route found, 3 a stretch worked out, 7 both ends in one sector, 6 a door in the way, 1 done. Left: 2. The grid's tests, a blocked start or goal and spawning are in `dev/PATHFINDING.md` (2026-10-18).

## 3. Dialogue

- **Why native:** scripts run inside a conversation's callbacks, and conversations are authored in the toolset. Changing a conversation at run time (a line, a choice, a condition) is out of their reach.
- **Leads:** `GameModeDialog`; the dialogue's messages (`SelectResponseMessage`); the conversation resource, a GFF file (`tools/gff4.py` reads the format).
- **Done when:** a plugin sees each line and choice as it is shown, and can add or hide a choice.
- **Done in code, not yet seen in play (2026-09-30):** `dao/Dialogue.h`. The engine's conversation object holds its file (GFF4 `CONV`), and a line is offered only if `Conversation::IsLineAvailable` (`0x007F0D30`) passes it, so `OnLineAvailable` can hide any line or offer one whose condition fails. `OnNpcLine` (on `PlayLine`, `0x007F1EF0`) and `OnReplies` (after dialogue mode's `ShowResponses`, `0x00690B70`) see what is spoken and offered. The lines are read from the file by a GFF4 reader in `core/` (`Gff4.h`, `Conversation.h`), tested, and checked against `tools/gff4.py` on 104 of the game's conversations. `examples/dialogue` logs a conversation. Since: choosing a reply and skipping a cutscene (`dao::dialogue::Choose`, `SkipCutscene`) and who speaks to whom (`Speaker`, `Listener`, `Owner`) seen in play through the Eluvian server. Left: hiding or adding a line seen in play; adding a line the file does not have, which would mean giving the engine a changed file.

## 4. The light on trees, grass and buildings

- **Why native:** an area's sky and sunlight can be set (`dao/Atmosphere.h`, DAOLib #62), and with them the ground, the creatures, the shadows and the haze follow from day to night. The trees, the grass and the buildings do not: under the night village's sky and sunlight the day village's pines and grass keep their warm olive on blue ground, and the night village's mill and trees stay night-dark on day-coloured ground. No script command or data table reaches any of it.
- **Leads:** the static models' lightmaps (`EnvModelLightmapAtlas`, field 3323, and the level's `LvlLightmap*`); the trees' controller (`EnvAreaTreecontrollerList`, 3355); the area file's `EnvLightProbe` list (3103, 3104, 3108) and the models' `MMHLightprobeIrradianceCoefficients*` (6190 to 6192); `EnvAreaSunlightColorMult` (3153), pushed nowhere in the exe, so inferred never read (`dev/EFFECTS.md`, "What a draw takes"). The layout's load (`EC::ChunkManager`, 0x005D1290) is where the sunlight's fields were found.
- **The trees, read 2026-10-16 (DAOLib #147):** SpeedTree's shaders take `g_vLightDir`, `g_vLightDiffuse` and `g_vColorMultiplier`, which the tree renderer binds by name to its own +0xBA8, +0xBB8 and +0xC5C (0x0052FB60 on). 0x00530DB0 copies +0xBA8 and +0xBB8 from the globals 0x00C31718 and 0x00C31728. 0x005C89B0 sets those globals as the area loads, where it makes the SpeedTreeLibrary: the direction is the scene's sun turned round and the diffuse is the sun's colour (0x004625D0 reads both from the light manager), or (0.577, 0.577, 0.577) and nothing without a sun. That is why the trees keep the light of the area's load: nothing writes the globals after it. `g_vColorMultiplier` is a tree's own tint (0x0052E0B8, from its +0x50). The trees have no ambient term at all. `dao::atmosphere::SetTreeLight` (DAOLib #148) writes both globals, the direction through SpeedTree's own setter (0x0052D6D0), and `SetSunlight` sets them with the sunlight. Not yet tried in play: that the trees follow the next frame, and that the direction's sign is right (the API's is the way the light travels, minus the global's).
- **The frame's colour curve, read 2026-10-17 (DAOLib #155):** the frame's last pass, da_hsvmatrix's `hslmatrix.psh`, takes the picture through a colour matrix and then each channel through a curve on sampler s1, the material system's internal parameter `ColorCurveTexture` (usage 0x32, `FUN_00509870`'s table). The curve is an area's own texture: `FUN_0067AFD0` reads Area Data (table 225, `areadata.gda`) at the row `CCArea+0x6B4` holds, the area's local int `AREA_ID` (set where the area is read back, `FUN_006882C0`), takes its `ColorCurves` (column hash 0xB3CBA486) into `CCArea+0x6B8`, and writes the row's `HSVBrightness`, `HSVContrast`, `HSVHue` and `HSVSaturation` over da_hsvmatrix's Brightness, Contrast, Hue and Saturation, so the row's values and not the level layout's are what the matrix gets. The name is a texture's: `cc_<area>.dds` in `textures.erf`, 210 of them, each 256 x 1 A8R8G8B8 (1,152 bytes with its header), each channel its own curve. Seen in play through the probe's watch on the device (`src/plugins/probe/ColourCurve.h`, vanilla, only Eluvian and the probe loaded): the texture bound on s1 is 256 x 1, format 21, managed, and its texels are `cc_bhm200.dds`'s byte for byte in the Circle tower (Witch Hunt's library), `cc_bhn100.dds`'s in Return to Ostagar's `kcc100ar_ostagar`; the ini's `Gamma` (0.5 and 1.0) left it as it was. `bhm100ar_tower_level_1` (level `lak303d`) is AREA_ID 54, row 54, `cc_bhm100`, and saturation 0.8. Of 240 areas, 23 name a row with no curve and one a row the table lacks; what is bound for them is not read (`cc_default.dds` is the identity, give or take one).
- **Done when:** a plugin can scale or tint the light trees, grass and buildings show, and the day village's pines go dark under the night village's look as the night village's own are.

## Done

Read, built and seen in play; what is left of each is small, and kept here.

- **Effects: duration and time left** (`CGameEffect::Duration`, `Remaining`, `SetRemaining`, `dao/Structures.h`; `dao::effects::On`, `Hold`, `Advance`, `OnAgeing`, `dao/Effects.h`; seen 2026-10-24, DAOLib #253). A daze applied for 12.5 s read a duration of 12.5 s and its time left fell, 9.86, 7.26, 4.68 s, as the game ran (through the Eluvian server's `creature`, which reads them); the turn-based mod's play tests 26 and 33 read a 6 s and a 2 s stun and a spider's 6.1 s Overwhelm as applied, and its log their time left falling turn by turn. Left: `SetRemaining` in play, `OnAgeing` given the object the effect is on (AgeEffect, `0x007A0B10`, has it as its first argument, and AddElapsed does not), and an example.
- **A blow that does not land far from the camera** (`dao::world::CreatureShowDistance`, `SetCreatureShowDistance`, DAOLib #88, seen 2026-10-12; asked by the multiplayer mod, whose host reckons every blow and whose guests' members may be far from the host's camera). The game hides a creature more than 60 m from the camera (30 to 60 by the graphics detail, which sets the distance each time it is applied, so the library holds a plugin's): faded out and taken out of the scene, it has no animation, and an attack's impact is an event of its animation. The reading is in `dao/Addresses.h`, "Where a creature's animation events come from". Three leads read first were each refuted in play, by the multiplayer mod's session and by the probe: the rooms' streaming, the creature's AI level (which `SetAILevel` does pin), and the scene's fade of far objects. Left: the distance is the game's one, not a creature's; a creature shown per id would need the pass at 0x006184E0 detoured. And past some 124 m the room a far creature stands in is unloaded, which was not tried.
- **A new object under an id of the plugin's choosing** (`dao::objects::CreateAs`, `LastNewId`, `NumberNewFrom`, `OnAreasBuild`, `dao/ObjectIds.h`, DAOLib #86 and #87, seen 2026-10-12; asked by the multiplayer mod at the user's question "Can we not set the ID for creatures so that they're properly synced?"). The reading is in `dao/Addresses.h`, "How an object gets its id". Left: the five other callers that register objects, by kind; an item under a chosen id, a creature's items being made by its template and numbered by the game; a word to a plugin as a creature is made, which the id's hook cannot give, the object being half constructed there.
- **The hang logger: a room's streaming told from a hang** (DAOLib #20, seen 2026-10-05): each sample's state line gives the rooms wanted and not loaded and those loading at the last frame, and a stall's end is the first frame back. Two stalls in the Dead Trenches showed both; one room owed is that area's steady state, so the rooms loading is the count to read.
- **A creature's size** (`dao::world::ModelBoxOf`, DAOLib #21, seen 2026-10-04): the box the renderer keeps for a creature's model, its meshes' bounds in the model's rest pose. Seen for four of the party, an ogre, a bronto, a deepstalker and a giant spider: the long bodies' lengths as drawn, a person's width arms out, an ogre's height upright. The model file's own `bbox` chunk and the PhysX shapes were looked at and left. The high dragon's (2026-10-05) is 31 by 22 m and 14.8 m high, its wings spread, where its body is 8 to 10 m. Left: a box in the creature's current pose, or of its body without the wings, if a plugin needs one.
- **Combat at the scripts' boundary** (`CommandCall::SetFloat`, `examples/damage`, seen 2026-10-04): the party's damage halved where the scripts hand the engine the new health, beside the turn-based mod and the Eluvian server. Left: a killing blow, which the scripts turn into a death effect instead, and two plugins changing the same command at once in play.
- **Input and key bindings** (`dao/Input.h`, 2026-10-01): a plugin's action, bound in the controls screen. Left: a held key (a callback on the release) and the mouse's codes.
- **Time: the game's speed and the clock** (`dao::clock::SetSpeed`, `dao/Clock.h`, `dao/Timers.h`, 2026-09-30 to 10-04). The reading of the three clocks is in `dao/Addresses.h` and the git history.
- **A plugin's own strings** (`dao/Strings.h`, 2026-09-30). Left: the female forms (`_f.tlk`), and adding to the engine's tables if a plugin's text must reach the game's own UI by strref.

## Also

- **A plugin's element over a game screen** (asked 2026-10-05 by the turn-based mod, its issue 46): a checkbox of the game's own art with a caption on the tactics screen, following the party member it shows, and a click heard. To read: whether the tactics movie exposes its selected character, and whether a child clip can be attached to it at run time without changing its file (dao::gui::OnMovieLoad patches a movie's bytes as it opens, as the options screen's section is added).
- **A portrait kept as a picture** (read 2026-10-05 for the turn-based mod's portraits, parked at the user's word: a live render per appearance, nothing kept on disk). Saving: the game imports `D3DXSaveTextureToFileW` and `D3DXSaveSurfaceToFileW` (d3dx9_36.dll; it writes each save's screen.dds), and a portrait is a 146x146 render target the texture manager made by name (`0x00847330`, its singleton `0x008472C0`), so a save wants name to texture to IDirect3DTexture9. Loading: the movies' image loader (`EclipseGFxImageLoader::LoadImage`, `0x00516A20`) takes `img://FILE:<path>` as a file on disk (`0x00846990`: .dds, .tga, .png or .jpg by its extension), so a picture needs no registering to be shown. Other `img://` names go to the texture manager by name (`0x00845D80`), after the interface atlases' named sub-images (`0x00523580`).
- **An effect in another colour.** `dao::vfx::Place`'s tint and `SetTint` reach the engine's own call (the model's slot 69) and showed nothing on the aiming ring in two play tests, 2026-10-01 (`dao/Vfx.h`). To try: a tint on a row whose model has an emitter (93052, an aura with no sound), where the game's own Tint column shows. To read: what gives a mesh's shader `mml_vUserColorMultiplier` when it is drawn. A reticle in another colour without either: a copy of the model's hierarchy naming a material object of its own (a few lines of text naming another texture), and a `vfx_` table in the override folder with a row for it, which needs a GDA writer.
